Write one document with role notes that a student can use to learn the attached material and work through its central ideas independently.

Follow the conceptual progression of the sources. Put prerequisites before the ideas that use them. Use topic headings that say what the section teaches. When combining sources, identify each by its exact label where its material first enters the discussion.

Teach each major idea in connected prose. Explain what problem it addresses, what it means, and why it works. Then give the precise definition, rule, or claim, including any condition that changes when it applies. Use lists for steps or compact reference facts. Give the important explanation room to breathe.

Use the parts of a LaTeX-style lecture note that suit the topic. For a theorem, distinguish the statement, hypotheses, and proof or proof idea. For a calculation, introduce the symbols, show the step where the method is chosen, and explain what the result means. For an experiment, connect the question, observation, and conclusion. Give reasons for non-obvious steps. Identify a proof sketch as a sketch.

Give a worked example for each substantial new method or difficult distinction. Prefer a source example when it illustrates the idea well. Otherwise label a small constructed case “Illustrative example” and identify any hypothetical inputs. Show enough of the process for the student to repeat it. For a conceptual topic, a concrete case with a sentence explaining why it fits can do the same job. Closely related concepts can share an example.
